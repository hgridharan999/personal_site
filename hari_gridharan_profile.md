## 11. How Hari Thinks — Mindset & Philosophy

This is drawn directly from his blog posts, LinkedIn writing, and the patterns across all his work.

### The Neuroplasticity Principle
His first blog post centers on neuroplasticity — the brain's ability to rewire itself — as philosophical proof that change is always possible. His takeaways are deeply personal:

1. "I should never stop pursuing whatever I want, because only growth and change can come out of it. This means that I continue doing the things I love and especially the things I know I should do, especially when I am demotivated or burnt out."
2. "I should never give up on my own ability to change for the better. It means that literally everyone has the capacity for growth at every stage and every moment of their life."

He explicitly invokes Bojack Horseman — a character who believes people cannot fundamentally change — and argues neuroplasticity disproves him.

### The Solipsism Framework for Risk-Taking
His second blog post argues for using the philosophical idea of solipsism — that you are the only truly real person — as a justification to take every possible risk:

- "I choose to look at it as rationale for why I should simply take every single risk I ever encounter."
- "One of the greatest barriers to success is inaction. One of the greatest reasons behind inaction is paralysis of public opinion."
- "If the only thing holding me back is public opinion, then why should I even care — especially when that public may not even be real?"
- "With a bit more delusion and a bit more confidence in our lives, we'd end up doing way more than we could ever imagine."
- "Failing and getting stuck is worse than getting stuck before you even start."

This is not performative — the same philosophy shows up in how he built Commit in 3 days, how he reframed his YC rejection, and how he ships projects constantly.

### The Story > Skills Belief
One of his most-engaged LinkedIn posts ("Have a story.") lays out a core belief:

- "You might be the most skilled person on the planet. None of it matters unless you can explain it to people."
- "Passion shines through 10x more than just hard work. And that passion is unique to you. That's what sets you apart from everyone else: the why, not the what."
- "Your story is the only thing that can't be copied, optimized, or outcompeted."

He applies this to himself — building in public, posting about failures, bringing his personality into professional work.

### Failure as Signal, Not Verdict
His YC rejection post (205+ reactions — his most viral content) is the clearest window into how he processes setbacks:

- "Even this rejection gives me clarity, maybe even more than an acceptance could."
- "It's an indicator that I'm on the right path."
- "Our first time founding and building, resulting in a business good enough for top 10% in the batch — it's proof. Proof that I should continue to build. Continue to fail. Continue to learn."
- "Be on the lookout — Henrik Gombos and I will be back soon."
