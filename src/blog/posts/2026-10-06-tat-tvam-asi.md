---

In Sanskrit, tat tvam asi translates to "that thou art". This saying originates from one of the core texts of Hinduism, the Upanishads. A sage tells his son that his innermost self is already one with the ultimate reality; that no matter what he does from here on out, that ultimate reality was already part of him. It's a demonstration that all matter in the universe is connected by this "invisible" essence that runs through all of us, governing much of Hinduism. I choose to read it differently. The opposite of the intended meaning is more interesting to ponder and more relevant to our time.

Choosing ones own interpretation is one of the great strengths of religion. One of the draws towards any kind of religion for me is the ability to adopt a personalized set of values that help govern and bring stability to your life. For example, my own belief in Hinduism and religion has constantly waxed and waned with time. I was raised to be devoted, and like many others, I rejected what was forced upon me. However, years of introspection later, I have come to accept and deny certain parts of Hinduism overall, and tat tvam asi is one of the ones I deny most heavily.

Choosing one's beliefs is highly important in a day and age when we are constantly bombarded with information and new perspectives - something that helps you keep grounded in the beliefs you hold dear. We're all pushed the same content, same information, and same careers, thanks to social media and technology that literally exists just to predict the next most likely word. Social media and large language models, and the homogeneity that they bring, strip us of having any individual claim to our thoughts. It seems that every "original" thought we have was just something that we picked up from a Claude chat, Instagram reel, or X feed. Algorithms designed to predict the average and to continue to push things that you even slightly interact with force everyone into the center of the Bell curve of all ideas.

It extends past model architecture and algorithms. Our thoughts, words, personalities, and appearances are constantly being judged by others, creating this need to satisfy all those around us. This forces us to be indecisive, compromising, and to be friends with everyone. But that also means that your opinions and beliefs are innately weak. The result is a greater lack of conviction in society. Everyone is striving towards something, but there's less clarity about what that something actually looks like on a personal level. That's why the opposite meaning of tat tvam asi is important.

I interpret the saying itself as you can be anything that you envision, and the only thing stopping you from becoming that thing is your own actions. This internally makes sense to some level. Obviously, we are all only limited by the amount and quality of work that we are able to put into any given task or skill at any given time, and luck and circumstances also factor in to some degree. However, this symbolizes more than that. Fundamentally, this signals that you need conviction. You need the conviction to choose a path and commit to it. All you are is that conviction. If you have the conviction to be the greatest swimmer, coder, hiker, writer, thinker on the planet, the only thing limiting you after that is truly the amount of work left to do. The most important step is to take the conviction to cut choices. Once you do that, you create this strong vision that you can actually work towards embodying.

I think the solution is to have conviction. You have to sit down and think about why you want the things you want, and then decide what success even looks like once you attain that something. Everyone lacks conviction to some degree. No one is going to be 100% certain about what they want. But building your conviction through introspection will set you up to be whatever you wish to be. That thou art = you are the sum of your beliefs.
