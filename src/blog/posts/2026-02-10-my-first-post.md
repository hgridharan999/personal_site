I've rebuilt my personal site more times than I'd like to admit. Each version gets more elaborate than the last — more animations, more decorations, more scrolling. This time I wanted to fight that impulse and actually ship something that loads fast and gets out of the way.

## The Problem with Complexity

The previous version had ink splatters, polaroid photo frames, hand-drawn SVG borders, and a journal spine in the middle of the page. It looked cool, but every time I opened it I thought: *this is a lot*. Visitors had to scroll through a full-screen hero before seeing anything meaningful about what I've actually built.

## What I Kept

The warm paper background stays. The handwritten fonts stay. The core idea of something that feels personal rather than corporate — that stays. I just stripped everything that didn't earn its place.

## What I'm Trying Now

One viewport. Name, a line about who I am, links to the things I've made. That's it. If you're curious, you click. If not, you've seen everything in three seconds.

We'll see how long this version lasts.
